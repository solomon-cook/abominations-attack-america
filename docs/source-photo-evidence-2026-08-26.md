### National Guard

The photographed record states that National Guard units may be placed in any
space with an unstomped city, base, or Infamy site. It shows these printed
statistics:

| Unit | Printed Move | Terrain | Defense | Damage |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Tank | 0 | — | 4 | 1 |
| Fighter | 0 | — | 3 | 1 |

Both records state that only the player with the Guard Commander card can move
Guard units. The photograph therefore supports the existing interpretation
that the printed Move 0 values are the neutral baseline, while the Guard
Commander card supplies the controllable movement values. It does not resolve
the card's full visibility, removal, or redeployment lifecycle beyond the
legible text recorded in the component catalogue.

### Coordinate-reference correction

The red row/column marks used during visual review are lattice references and
can fall on a shared vertex or edge between faces. For example, the Portland
panel is surrounded by the nearby `0/2`, `1/1`, and `2/2` references; those
marks are not three visitable spaces and none of them alone proves the panel's
cell. The transcription convention is therefore: identify the hex face that
contains the printed panel, assign that face's generated cell ID, and retain
surrounding lattice marks only as alignment evidence. The generated comparison
overlay now labels face centres and records the surrounding geometry warning
in its accessible description.

The current comparison overlay is intentionally a clean mesh-only audit
surface. Earlier provisional feature and barrier annotations were cleared
after the flat-top correction; they must be re-added only after the relevant
face is checked against this mesh.

The current diagnostic registration is recorded as image-space flat-top face
centres: `x = 220 + 49.5 * column` and `y = 65 + 57.2 * (row + 0.5 on odd
columns)` on the clockwise-rotated 1600 x 900 source view. Faces use a 66 px
face width and approximately 57.2 px face height; barrier diagnostics use the
two exact shared vertices of neighbouring faces. These constants define the
review aid, not a claim that every printed seam has been physically verified.
Future feature promotion must check the panel against this face, not a nearby
label or shared vertex.
