## Resolved by source review

| Gate | Evidence and resolution |
| --- | --- |
| Laser Fence and Chopper Lift | The photographed cards say Laser Fence may be used “at any time when a monster ends its move, but before battle,” while Chopper Lift says to “move a monster up to that many spaces.” The engine treats the completed Chopper Lift movement as opening that printed Laser Fence window. This is a literal wording interpretation; neither card explicitly names the other. The regression checks that retreating a different monster leaves the active monster's pending Encounter intact. Sources: [Laser Fence](../references/monsters-menace-america/components/decks/military-research-01.jpg), [Chopper Lift](../references/monsters-menace-america/components/decks/military-research-03.jpg), and `packages/game-engine/src/index.test.ts`. |
| Blonde Lure and Laser Fence | Blonde Lure's required Move endpoint is reached when the monster ends its Move on the chosen space. Laser Fence's card opens only after that event (“when a monster ends its move, but before battle”), so its forced retreat is a later displacement and does not undo the fulfilled endpoint; the printed card also says the retreat does not encounter its new space. This is a literal timing reading, not text that explicitly names the other card. The engine regression verifies the monster reaches the lured space, opens the Laser Fence window, then retreats without restoring the lure or triggering a new Encounter. Sources: [Blonde Lure](../references/monsters-menace-america/components/decks/military-research-02.jpg), [Laser Fence](../references/monsters-menace-america/components/decks/military-research-01.jpg), and `packages/game-engine/src/index.test.ts`. |
| Blonde Lure and Chopper Lift | Blonde Lure constrains the selected monster's next turn. Chopper Lift can move that monster on an earlier cardholder turn, so the lift neither satisfies nor consumes the lure; the selected endpoint remains required on the next Move when reachable. The audited-board engine regression confirms the lure survives Chopper Lift and constrains the later move while an ordinary alternative destination exists. Sources: [Blonde Lure](../references/monsters-menace-america/components/decks/military-research-02.jpg), [Chopper Lift](../references/monsters-menace-america/components/decks/military-research-03.jpg), and `packages/game-engine/src/index.test.ts`. |
| Blonde Lure and Molecular Cannon | Blonde Lure requires the selected monster to end its Move on the chosen adjacent space during its next turn. Molecular Cannon relocates the monster to a lair at the start of a battle before that turn; this earlier displacement does not satisfy or consume the lure. The audited-board regression confirms the selected endpoint remains required after the relocation when it is reachable, while another ordinary destination exists. Sources: [Blonde Lure](../references/monsters-menace-america/components/decks/military-research-02.jpg), [Molecular Cannon](../references/monsters-menace-america/components/decks/military-research-02.jpg), and `packages/game-engine/src/index.test.ts`. |
| Antimatter and Molecular Cannon | Both cards have a start-of-battle window. Using Molecular Cannon first removes the battle, so Antimatter can no longer be used there and remains in hand. If Antimatter is used first, it is discarded and arms first-round damage; a subsequent Molecular Cannon removes the battle before any combat round, so the Antimatter modifier has no effect. Engine regressions cover both orders. Sources: [Molecular Cannon](../references/monsters-menace-america/components/decks/military-research-02.jpg), [Antimatter](../references/monsters-menace-america/components/decks/military-research-03.jpg), and `packages/game-engine/src/index.test.ts`. |
| Atomic Breath and Berserk | Atomic Breath adds one attack in the first combat round of each battle. Berserk adds five attacks when discarded during a battle. If Berserk is used before the first round resolves, both independently printed attack grants apply; the engine regression checks seven first-round attacks from a one-attack base and no added Atomic Breath attack in round two. Sources: [Atomic Breath](../references/monsters-menace-america/components/decks/monster-mutation-02.jpg), [Berserk](../references/monsters-menace-america/components/decks/monster-mutation-01.jpg), and `packages/game-engine/src/index.test.ts`. |

The typed records are implemented and covered by the deployment matrix; see [`branch-deployment-audit.md`](branch-deployment-audit.md). |

The deployed Guard piece remains in the neutral inventory; see [`national-guard-control-audit.md`](national-guard-control-audit.md). |

## Reply format

For each row above that remains open, provide either a source page/photo and the relevant ruling, or mark the question as unknown. For the card row, answers may be limited to the specific interactions the source establishes; unresolved combinations remain gated. Do not infer a rule from a card name, theme, or another game's convention.

Once evidence arrives, update the typed source record and the associated test first, then update the single release-facing status in `unresolved-rules-inventory.md`. Board visual and independent review remain human acceptance steps even when every board field passes machine validation.
