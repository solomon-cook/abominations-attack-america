# Deliberate digital adaptations

These decisions describe the digital product boundary. They are not tabletop rules and must not change the authoritative engine outcome.

| Area | Digital decision | Engine/UI consequence | Verification needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Card visibility | Mutation and Research cards are placed face up in front of their owner when drawn; their identities are public to players and spectators, including both temporary Toxicor choices | Projections expose public `visibleMutationCardIds`/`visibleResearchCardIds`, pending choice faces, and public event identities, while private owner arrays, deck order, and discard contents stay redacted; only the Toxicor owner can choose | Original rulebook p. 13; engine, Memory/Prisma room, spectator, event, and Toxicor UI fixtures cover the visibility and authority boundaries |
| Random stream | Shuffle order and die outcomes must remain deterministic for authoritative replay without exposing future outcomes to room participants | Newly created non-fixture rooms store a 256-bit private server seed and use the RFC 8439 ChaCha20 block function with disjoint counter ranges for rolls, deck shuffles, and returned Mutation cards; public projections redact the seed and cursor | RFC 8439 Appendix A.1 test vector, deterministic private-seed engine fixture, and player/spectator projection tests; old snapshots without `secretSeed` continue on the legacy deterministic stream |
| Simultaneous choices | If a rules step needs simultaneous input, collect named pending decisions and resolve only when the required actors have submitted | Commands identify actor and pending decision; UI shows waiting state | Pending-decision and reconnect tests |
| Dice presentation | Dice outcomes are generated by the authoritative engine/RNG and recorded in events; UI animates or displays the recorded result | No client-side roll decides a rule outcome | Seeded replay test and browser result check |
| Disconnect handling | Reconnect restores the server revision and pending decision; stale clients reconcile instead of replaying an accepted action | Commands carry action ID and expected revision | Refresh, stale-revision, polling, and WebSocket QA |
| Accessibility | Motion, colour, audio, and decorative imagery are supplementary; every required action has text and keyboard/screen-reader semantics | State and legality are represented as data and labels | Keyboard, screen-reader, contrast, reduced-motion, and zoom review |
| Voluntary concession | The active player may confirm a concession; the next seat is recorded as the winner and the match becomes terminal | `concede` is an authoritative command, emits `match.conceded`, persists through room snapshots, and is visible in the terminal summary | Engine and store tests plus reconnect/idempotency coverage |
| Stabilizer Ray target timing | Arm the card at battle start; after the first military damage, choose among the monster's Mutations present at that damage event | Eligible face-up Mutation IDs are captured on the battle, then the cardholder's choice is persisted as a decision and the prior battle or retreat decision resumes after discard | Engine fixtures cover post-damage choice, no prompt on a miss, discard lifecycle, and decision resumption; authenticated Memory and Prisma tests cover refresh |

- The development fixture preserves the source Stomp count in state but may end early with `development-board-exhaustion` when every space in the intentionally smaller abstract board has been stomped. This terminal condition is disabled for production rulesets and exists only to keep the first-playable fixture finishable without allowing duplicate stomps.
- Card lifecycle primitives keep the authoritative shuffled order, draw cursor, discard list, and exhaustion flag in state. Drawing reveals the card face to every player, as the rulebook requires; projections redact the authoritative deck order and discard contents. No card effect is applied by these primitives.

## Scope rule

An adaptation is accepted only when it is documented here, does not make the UI authoritative, and has an engine or browser verification path. Unknown tabletop facts remain in [the unresolved inventory](unresolved-rules-inventory.md).
