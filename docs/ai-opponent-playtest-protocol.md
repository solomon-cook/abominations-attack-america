# AI opponent difficulty playtest

## Purpose

Check whether players experience the Casual, Tactical, and Challenging profiles as distinct, fair difficulty levels. This complements deterministic bot self-play; it does not measure win-rate balance or establish a statistically validated ranking.

## Session

1. Invite players who have not tuned or implemented the bot. Tell them the goal is to evaluate the opponent, not their skill. Do not collect names, account details, or other personal data.
2. Have each player complete one solo match against each profile, using the same board and game rules. Vary the profile order between players so the first or last match does not always use the same profile.
3. Let the player use the normal controls and rules help. Do not advise strategy during play. Record whether a match completes, elapsed time, and any point where the bot's action is confusing or seems unfair.
4. After each match, ask the player to rate challenge, fairness, and enjoyment from 1 (low) to 5 (high), and to name the most and least difficult opponent so far. Ask for one short reason behind each rating.
5. After all three matches, ask whether the profiles felt distinct, which profile they would choose for an easier game, and whether any action seemed illegal or inexplicable.

## Readout

Report the number of participants and completed matches, profile order, rating distributions, match completion and duration, repeated confusion or fairness issues, and representative anonymized comments. Keep individual responses unlinked to identities. Treat the results as directional; do not claim the difficulty curve is validated if ratings overlap materially or players report unclear or illegal bot behavior.

## Status

Protocol only. No player sessions or results are implied by this document.
